# Warm register: drafting toolkit and named failure modes

Open this once a draft exists and reads wrong, or when reviewing someone else's warm piece.

## Drafting toolkit - held loosely

These are what good material reaches for on its own, not techniques to install while drafting.

- **The telling particular** - one precise, slightly surprising *how*. The strongest device, and usually where any humor already lives.
- **The refusal** - the profitable default they turned down. **Load-bearing at most once per piece, and at most once across a batch.** It is the fastest-fingerprinting move in this register: six of seven pieces in one real batch closed on it, which is how the pattern was caught.
- **The world that can't parse it** - deadpan bafflement at the subject from outside, amused rather than indignant. If an "everyone else" line reads as an accusation, it belongs in adversarial coverage of *them*.
- **The uncommented quote** - real, slightly odd, left to stand. Commentary after a warm quote puts the writer between the reader and the person.
- **Stated at its real scale** - a small thing said plainly. The honesty of the scale is part of what makes it land.
- **The endearing flaw** - a small, real oddity. Apparent flawlessness reads as invented even when true; a minor imperfection makes the rest credible, as long as it stays texture.

## Named failure modes

- **Press-release curdle.** Direct praise, launch-copy cadence, "no catch" energy. Test: would the subject's PR team publish this unchanged? Its most common surface is flattering-descriptor vocabulary (*inspiring, heartwarming, remarkable, fascinating*) - a widely noted machine-writing tell, and in this register a claim asked on faith where a concrete detail would be evidence. Cut the adjective, keep the detail.
- **The manufactured target.** A jab at "everyone else" with no villain in the verified record. Test: is the sentence amused or indignant? Indignant means it is adversarial copy in this register's clothes.
- **The vanishing subject.** The person becomes a doorway into a broader essay and stops being the grammatical subject. Test: are they the subject of most sentences, and of the closer?
- **The backhanded kicker.** Praise that imports the deficit or doubt it claims to reject.
- **Halo inflation.** The overstated version of a true good deed (hard rule 4).
- **Sainthood.** No oddity or texture anywhere; reads as invented even when every fact is true.
- **Solemnity creep.** Memorial gravity leaking into a piece about someone alive and active. Warm is not hushed.
- **The moral.** A closer that names the virtue - the narrator explaining the joke.

## Batch-level checks

Warm material run through the same machinery converges fast. Check a batch as a set, not piece by piece:

- **Negation monoculture.** More than one closer leaning on "no X, no Y - just Z" is a fingerprint, even if each piece reads fine alone.
- **Convergence.** Several pieces sharing a closer move, a sentence shape, or a warmth-signalling phrase. The fix is Step 1 - a genuinely different true sentence per piece - never a new rhythm rule bolted onto the existing ones.
