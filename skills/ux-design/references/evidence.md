## Usability heuristics

- **Source:** Jakob Nielsen, "10 Usability Heuristics for User Interface Design", Nielsen Norman Group (nngroup.com).
- **What was read (2026-11-12):** its summary line, and the opening of four of the ten heuristics: the first (visibility of system status), the third (user control and freedom), the fifth (error prevention) and the sixth (recognition rather than recall). The other six, and the articles each heuristic links to, were not read.
- **What those parts say:** the heuristics are broad rules of thumb, not specific guidelines. A design keeps users informed of what is going on; it gives a clearly marked way out of an unwanted action, with undo and redo; it prevents errors before they happen, and tells two kinds apart, slips (unconscious, from inattention) and mistakes (conscious, from a mental model that does not match the design); and it does not make the user hold in memory what an earlier screen showed.
- **Where it is used:** `SKILL.md` section 2. The four questions of the walk-through restate those four heuristics for a wireframe, and the slip and mistake bullet uses the distinction. The remedies given there are this skill's own. On one point the skill is narrower than the source: the source's tips for mistakes include warning the user and a confirmation before they commit, and this skill accepts a warning only when it says what will happen.
- **How far it reaches:** a practitioner's list of rules of thumb; not a measurement.
