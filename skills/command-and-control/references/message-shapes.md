# Message shapes: command-and-control

Open this when writing a choice for the owner, a relay of the owner's words, or a ruling. Each slot has a case in `evidence.md` behind it, named under the shape. Fill every slot. Where a slot has nothing to hold, write "none" in it (I: a written "none" is the writer's statement that there is nothing; a slot left out says nothing). Two lines are left out when they do not apply: the first line of shape 1 and the last line of shape 3.

## 1. A decision for the owner

```
<the step that has to happen first; no such line if there is none>

1. <the question, with what the owner needs to know to answer it>
   a) <what this option gives>
   b) <what this option gives>
   <every route that is really open, not only the one you would take; say so where routes can be combined>
   My pick: <one option, or several that can be combined>, because of <the result it gives>
   Scheduling: <what each option takes; never the reason for the pick>
```

- The first line is the precondition, never the "yes" (the case: a reply opened with "yes", the owner acted on it, and results were lost).
- One numbered question for each decision, outside any status report, with its context (the cases: decisions buried in a status message; a question about a feature nobody had explained).
- No option is left out because the coordinator would not take it, and options that can stand together are not offered as either-or (the case: three ways to end a run offered as if one excluded the others).
- The pick rests on what an option gives, and what each takes stands apart from it (the case: a recommendation that rested on less work, not on the better result).
- The owner answers with a letter or in their own words; a condition or another choice they add is recorded as written. The session then writes the ruling, and records it as the option's text plus the owner's answer, not as the owner's own sentence (the cases: the owner asked to word each answer; "your rule" said of an option text).
- A broken thing is not a question. It is fixed and reported in one line.
- The form of the options and of the effort line is `task-scoping` section 3.

## 2. A relay of the owner's words

Each brief gives this shape to its worker, for the moment the owner types in the worker's window.

```
The owner typed in my window (relayed): "<the owner's words, letter for letter>"
What it answered: <the question or the options, exactly as the owner saw them | nothing: the owner said it unasked>
Not said by the owner: <my reading of it, if I acted on one>
The owner's "done" on it: <given, in the words above | not given | the owner is still working on it with me>
```

- The words are quoted, never retold, and the options travel with the answer: an answer approves an option as it was worded (the case: "your rule").
- The last slot is there because a report is not the owner's "done" while the owner is still working in that window (the case: other sessions started from an unfinished draft). "Not given" is an answer of its own: the talk can end without a "done".
- A relay is not an approval for the session that receives it to do anything hard to undo. For that, it asks the owner directly (`context-and-handoff` section 7).

## 3. A ruling to a worker

```
<the fix, the go or the ruling>
Rests on: <the owner's words, quoted, with where they are recorded> | <my reading, and that it is mine>
Second reader: <what its reading changed> | no change        (only where the owner has set one)
```

- "Rests on" has two possible fillings and no third: the owner's words, or the session's reading named as such. A written rule of the project is cited with where it stands and with who worded it: the owner, or a session whose wording the owner kept. It is quoted as the owner's own sentence only where the owner wrote it.
- The last line is written after the reading, and never without one. Where the owner has set a second reader, a worker that receives a ruling without the line asks for it before acting.
