# Named failure modes - definitions, tests, fixes

The value of a name is that it turns "this reads wrong" into a specific thing to fix. Open this once a draft exists and reads wrong.

## Stance and structure

- **Polished emptiness.** Technically clean, correctly paced, nothing at stake. Fails distillation. Naming it doesn't prevent it; only having something to say does. Send it back to `drafting` Step 1.
- **The proven mechanism, cold.** The piece establishes exactly *how* something was done - the loophole, the circularity, the euphemism - and never says what is wrong with it. Distinct from emptiness: it has an argument and refuses to take its own side. **Test: would it read the same if the writer were indifferent?**
- **The double lede.** The subhead states the claim; the opening states the same claim in other words. **Not script-detectable** - the worst real case shared zero content words between the two and was still one proposition twice. Ask what a reader who just read the subhead *learns* from sentence one. "Nothing, phrased differently" is the failure. Fix the derivation (the body opens on evidence and arrives at the claim; `drafting` Step 4), not the sentence.
- **Report-shaped drafting.** A property of how the piece was made, and it causes three symptoms together. Writing the body lede-first - biggest fact in sentence one, descending - is right for a headline and wrong for an argument: the subhead gets lifted off the opening (double lede), and the ending becomes whatever fact is left when the list runs out. One real batch showed all three symptoms in all five pieces, from one drafting habit. **Cheap tell: the body's first sentence would work as the headline.**
- **Decorative cold open.** An opening that gestures at cleverness without entering the argument. Fails deletion. The fix is starting the argument at word one, not a better gesture.
- **The wall.** A body over ~70 words as one unbroken paragraph. On a narrow column it reads as work, but the deeper tell is structural: no break usually means no turn. **Don't fix it by inserting a break.** Find the turn, or admit there isn't one.

## The four ending failures

All four have one cause: the ending was derived from the argument instead of from the reader's reaction (`drafting` Step 3).

- **The trailing qualifier.** The sting lands mid-sentence and the writing continues past it. Cut back to the hit.
- **The abstract closer.** Ends on a concept - a calculation, a deterrent, paperwork - rather than a thing or a person. Concepts can't be pictured, so they can't land.
- **The unpacked closer.** One sentence of contempt spread across three. Unpacked, it reads as the piece arguing with itself.
- **The relieving closer.** An ending built from a fact that *resolves* the reader's feeling instead of withholding resolution - most often a lucky coincidence, which slips through because it is verified, concrete and moving. A real case: a piece about a hole torn in an aircraft cabin ended on the two seats beside it being empty. True, and exactly wrong, because "nobody was sitting there" is the relief the piece existed to withhold.

## Voice

- **Wryness instead of anger.** A wink where an accusation belongs. **Tell: it would still be funny if the facts were harmless.**
- **Newswire voice.** Filler that makes copy read machine-assembled: *a new report finds, amid growing concerns, sparks outrage, raises questions, in a statement, the move comes as, sources say, critics say, observers note.* The surrounding sentence is usually a stance-free summary; fix that, not just the phrase.
- **Wire diction pasted intact.** A true detail in the source's institutional phrasing. It reads as invented even when sourced. Particulars are evidence; put them in your own words.
- **Press-release voice on a warm piece.** Praise adjectives standing in for detail; see `good-news-writing`.
- **Retrospective-quote lede.** "X once said..." opens a profile, not news - old energy in a live feed.
- **Thin body on a real story.** A lead fact and a consequence, with no mechanism (*how* it happened) and no quote or document beat. It reads as a caption stretched to fill a field. The fix is a real beat from real research, never padding. A genuine notice is exempt.

## Batch level

- **Batch fingerprint.** Uniformity visible only across pieces: identical opening moves, shortest-sentence lengths clustering on one number, the same shape recurring, one verb owning every headline. Checked by script across the batch (`mechanical-checks.md`), and never fixed by writing to a rhythm target - the fix is a genuinely different Step-1 sentence per piece.
- **A recurring conclusion is not a fingerprint.** If the same finding keeps arriving because the material keeps producing it, that is the central claim accumulating. Vary how it lands; never whether it lands.
